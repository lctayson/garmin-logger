## Known risk factors (things worth flagging if seen, not generic praise)

1. **Anaerobic development history**: a prior 22-week HM block produced
   literally zero anaerobic load against target, despite nominally
   training for it. Watch for genuine VO2max-intensity stimulus actually
   landing in Tuesday sessions, not just sub-threshold volume.
2. **Race-day pacing discipline**: a prior half marathon attempt collapsed
   from early demoralization at slow-looking splits, well below actual
   fitness. The agreed countermeasure is a deliberate negative split and
   hiding average pace on the watch -- relevant context for any session
   that involves pacing under pressure (time trials, tune-up races).
3. Training load itself has rarely been the actual problem -- most issues
   have been sleep/recovery-driven (acute) or execution-driven (pacing,
   anaerobic stimulus), not overreaching from volume.

## What makes a good analysis here

- Specific to the data in front of you -- reference actual numbers
  (drift %, HR delta, compliance score, readiness factors), not generic
  encouragement.
- Say when something is unremarkable/as-expected, not just when something
  stands out -- false alarms erode trust as much as missed real signals.
- Connect to the current phase/goal when it's actually relevant; don't
  force a BHM 2027 reference into every single easy run.
- 2-4 sentences. This runs daily -- it should be worth reading each time,
  not a template with numbers swapped in.
